Everything here lives in **Settings → Account** (`/settings?tab=account`).
Account-security changes always happen in the instance's own web pages: a
[connected application](/using/connected-applications/) cannot make them for
you.

## Sign-in details

| Detail       | Notes                                                                                                   |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Username** | Your fediverse address, `@username@your-domain`. It is permanent and cannot be changed by anyone.       |
| **Email**    | Your login email. Shows **Verified** or **Unverified**; an unverified address can resend its link here. |
| **Password** | 12 to 128 characters.                                                                                   |

You can sign in with either your username or your email. When the instance has
the [leaked-password check](/reference/environment/#accounts) on (the default),
a password found in a known breach is refused. Only a short hash prefix leaves
the server for that check, never the password.

<Callout type="note" title="Why the username is permanent">
  Other servers store your follows, mentions and replies against `@username@your-domain`, and every profile and post
  link is built from it. Renaming it would break all of them, and a freed name could be claimed by someone else. Your
  **display name** can change freely in Settings → Profile.
</Callout>

## Changing your password

Choose **Change password**, then enter your current password and the new one.
Saving it signs out every other session, and a security notice goes to your
login email. If you did not make the change, follow the reset link in that email
immediately.

Forgot it? Use **Forgot password** on the sign-in page; the reset link goes to
your login email.

## Changing your email

**Change email** takes three steps:

1. **Confirm your password.** The change has to follow a fresh password check,
   within 15 minutes.
2. **Enter the new address.** A 6-digit code is sent there. It lasts 10 minutes
   and allows 5 attempts; you can request up to 3 codes a minute.
3. **Enter the code.** The email changes, and every other session is signed
   out.

The old address then receives a notice with an **undo link**, valid for 7
days. Use it if the change was not you: it puts the old address back and locks
the account down as if it were stolen. Every session ends, all passkeys are
removed, and the password is cleared. A password-reset link then goes to the
restored address so you can get back in.

<Callout type="warning" title="Undo is a lockdown, not a revert">
  Following the undo link also cancels any later email change, and you have to set a new password and add your passkeys
  again afterwards.
</Callout>

## Passkeys

Passkeys sign you in with your fingerprint, face, or screen lock instead of a
password. Add one under **Passkeys**; it is named after its provider (for
example "Windows Hello" or "1Password") when that is known, and you can
**Rename** it (up to 60 characters) or **Remove** it.

Your login email gets a notice whenever a passkey is added or removed. The
notice links back to this section so you can remove one you do not recognise.

## Active sessions

**Active sessions** lists every device signed in to your account, with your
current one marked. Sign out a single device, or **Sign out all other
sessions**. If you do not recognise a device, sign it out, then change your
password. Sessions otherwise last 30 days.

## Deleting your account

**Delete account** asks for your password and permanently deletes your account,
articles, and follows. See [Profiles](/using/profiles/#deleting-your-account)
for what happens to copies already delivered to other servers.
