The Omicron app for Android and iOS covers what the web app does: reading,
writing, following, lists, notifications, settings, and moderation. It connects
to one Omicron instance at a time and needs no Google Play Services.

## Choose an instance

On first launch, choose **omicron.blog** (the main instance) or select
**Another instance** and enter an address such as `example.com`. The app checks
that the server is an Omicron instance that has finished its setup before
connecting.

To switch, open **Settings → Change instance**. That signs you out of the
current instance. Each instance keeps its own session and saved data, and the
app never sends one instance's session to another.

## Sign in or create an account

Sign in with your **username or email** and password. Passkeys are not
supported in the app yet; sign in with your password.

To create an account, select **Sign in**, then create one with a username,
email address, and password. The username is permanent: it is your address on
the fediverse. Passwords are 12 to 128 characters. If the instance requires a
confirmed email, the app shows **Check your inbox**; open the link, then sign
in. **Resend confirmation link** sends a fresh one.

**Forgot password** sends a reset link to your email. If the instance cannot
send email yet, the app says so; ask its administrator instead.

## Navigate the app

Signed in, the bottom bar holds **Home**, **Lists**, **Write**, **Stats**, and
your **Profile**. Signed out, it holds **Home**, **Search**, and **Sign in**.

Home's header has **Notifications** (with the unread count) and **Search**.
Your avatar opens the account menu: **Your posts**, **Follow requests**,
**Settings**, and, for moderators and admins, **Moderation**.

## Read

Home shows **For you**, **Local**, and **Global**; choose which one it opens on
in **Settings → Preferences**. Open a post to read it, then like, recommend,
comment, share, or save it to a list. Search covers articles, people, and tags;
its **Surprise me** button opens a random post.

Links to profiles, posts, tags, and lists on the connected instance open
in the app; other links open in the browser.

On Android, `omicron.blog` publishes an Android App Links association for
`blog.omicron.app`. Android can route eligible HTTPS links to a compatible
installed app after verifying the domain. Other instances do not publish this
association by default; operators can configure it as described in
[Environment variables](/reference/environment/#android-app-links). Server-side
association alone does not verify an installed release's complete email-confirmation
or sign-in handoff.

## Comments

Comments are plain text, up to 2,000 characters. Reply to a comment, like it,
or edit and delete your own.

## Write and manage posts

**Write** opens the composer: a title and a rich-text body with headings,
lists, quotes, code blocks, tables, dividers, links, and images. **Story
settings** holds the banner, summary, tags, language, and the
[AI-assisted](/using/writing/) disclosure. Publish now, save as a draft, or
schedule for at least five minutes from now.

**Your posts** (in the account menu) has **Drafts**, **Scheduled**, and
**Published** tabs. From there you can open a post in the editor, publish a
scheduled post now, unschedule it, move a published post back to drafts, or
delete one. Deleting cannot be undone.

## Lists

**Lists** shows **Read later** and your own lists. Create a list with a title,
description, and an optional **Private list** switch. Save any post to a list
from the post itself. See [Lists and read later](/using/lists/).

## Stats

**Stats** is your [writer dashboard](/using/analytics/) over the last 7, 30, or
90 days: views (when the instance counts them), likes, comments, and new
followers, beside all-time totals and per-post figures.

## Settings

| Section          | What it holds                                                                                   |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Profile**      | Photo, name, bio, links, public email, topics, and the About section (Markdown, with a preview) |
| **Account**      | Email, password, signed-in devices, delete account                                              |
| **Privacy**      | Private account; muted and blocked accounts                                                     |
| **Preferences**  | Theme, the feed Home opens on, feed languages, AI-assisted posts, followed tags                 |
| **Integrations** | Publishing tokens and connected applications                                                    |

The **App** section links to the instance's About page, rules, and privacy
policy, the source code, and the licence, and offers **Open on the web**,
**Change instance**, and **Sign out**.

**Account** works like [Account and security](/using/account/) on the web:
change your email (with a 6-digit code) or password, sign out other devices, or
delete your account. **Passkeys** opens the instance's web settings, where
passkeys are managed.

## Moderation

Moderators and admins get **Moderation** in the account menu: open and resolved
reports, and the user list (all, admins, suspended, unverified) with search.
Resolve a report, optionally telling the author by email; remove a reported
post or suspend an account. A post's menu also offers moderators **Mark as
AI-assisted** or **Remove AI-assisted label**. Instance settings stay on the
web. See [Moderation tools](/using/moderation/).

## Offline and updates

Loaded posts, profiles, and lists can be kept on the device for up to 7 days
after their last successful update. Only the first two pages of paginated lists
are persisted. Offline, the app can show that saved data with a **You're offline**
banner and resumes when the connection returns. Search, moderation, and session
lists are not saved. A new app build, over-the-air update, or API contract version
invalidates the saved query cache.

When an instance runs a newer API than the app understands (its
[`apiVersion`](/reference/http-api/#reports-instance-setup-seo) is ahead), the
app shows a banner asking you to update it.

## Session security

The session is stored in the device's secure storage, separate for each
instance, and is only ever sent to the instance that issued it. Other app data
is kept in ordinary app storage and is excluded from Android backups.
