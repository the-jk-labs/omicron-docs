Federation is **off by default**, so a fresh instance is a private, standalone
blog. Turning it on lets your posts travel the fediverse and lets people on
Mastodon and elsewhere follow your writers.

## Order matters

Federation binds your server's identity to your **domain at startup**, so:

1. **Set your public domain first** — in the wizard or **Admin → Instance** — and
   confirm HTTPS works at `https://your-domain`. Federation effectively requires
   HTTPS; other servers will refuse plain HTTP.
2. **Turn on the Federation toggle** in **Admin → Instance** (or set
   `FEDERATION_ENABLED=true` in `.env`).
3. **Restart** so the change takes effect:

   ```bash
   docker compose up -d
   ```

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not change the domain afterwards">
  Every actor URI, key, and delivered activity is built from the domain. Changing it later orphans your existing
  followers — remote servers keep following an identity that no longer answers.
</Callout>

## Confirm it is active

On the server:

```bash
curl localhost:8000/version      # -> "federation": true
```

Publicly:

```bash
curl https://your-domain/.well-known/nodeinfo    # -> a links array
curl https://your-domain/nodeinfo/2.1            # -> software, version, usage
```

A 404 on either means federation is still off. The backend also logs
`Federation enabled (ActivityPub)` on boot.

The first of those is what fediverse directories fetch, so a working answer is
also how the instance gets listed — see
[NodeInfo](/federation/endpoints/#discovery).

Finally, the real test — search for `@yourusername@your-domain` from a Mastodon
account and follow it. You should see the follow appear, be auto-accepted, and
your next published post arrive in that account's timeline.

## What turning it on changes

- Each local account becomes an ActivityPub actor with an inbox and outbox.
- Inbound `Follow` activities are auto-accepted (unless the account requires
  approval for followers).
- Publishing a post enqueues delivery to every remote follower's inbox.
- Your instance answers WebFinger, host-meta, actor, followers, outbox, and
  NodeInfo requests. See [ActivityPub endpoints](/federation/endpoints/).
- It becomes visible to fediverse directories — FediDB, instances.social,
  fediverse.observer. They read NodeInfo, which publishes aggregate counts only:
  accounts, sign-ins in the last 30 and 180 days, published local posts, and
  responses.
- Each account gains a second, machine-facing address at `/users/{username}`
  alongside its `/@{username}` profile page.
- The federation inbox becomes a public, unauthenticated POST endpoint —
  rate-limited per source IP and capped in body size.
- The instance starts **caching the Omicron network** in the background, and
  contacts the FediDB directory once a day. See below.

## The federation network (auto-cache)

Without help, the Global feed only carries remote posts someone here already
looked up, followed, or saw boosted, so a new instance's Global feed starts
almost empty. The auto-cache network fills it ahead of your readers.

**Admin → Federation → Federation network** lists the _tracked instances_.
They come from two places:

| Source            | How it gets there                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **FediDB**        | Once a day, every Omicron instance listed by [FediDB](https://fedidb.com/software/omicron) is added |
| **Added by hand** | You type a domain into **Add instance**                                                             |

Each tracked instance is synced every **6 hours** by default:

1. Its NodeInfo is read to confirm it runs Omicron.
2. Its public local feed is read **only to learn who has written recently**, up
   to **20 writers** by default.
3. Each writer is resolved over ActivityPub, and up to 20 of their latest public
   posts are cached.

Posts are never copied from the other instance's API. They are fetched the same
way as when a reader opens a remote profile, so they go through the same checks:
only public posts, only from the author's own server, and never from blocked
domains or accounts.

| Setting                                  | Default | Range |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------- | ----- |
| **Auto-cache the network**               | On      | —     |
| **Discover Omicron instances on FediDB** | On      | —     |
| **Sync every (hours)**                   | 6       | 1–168 |
| **Writers per instance**                 | 20      | 1–100 |

Settings apply live, with no restart. Turning **Auto-cache the network** off
stops both syncing and discovery.

Each instance row shows its status, its NodeInfo summary, how many accounts and
posts are cached from it, and when it last synced and will sync next:

| Status             | Meaning                                                                                                        |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Waiting**        | Not synced yet                                                                                                 |
| **Synced**         | The last sync worked                                                                                           |
| **Other software** | Not Omicron. Writers can't be discovered there; follows and replies still federate normally. Rechecked weekly. |
| **Failing**        | The last sync failed. The error is shown, and retries back off up to once a week.                              |
| **Skipped**        | The domain is suspended or limited under Domain blocks                                                         |
| **Paused**         | You switched it off                                                                                            |

- **Pause** an instance with its switch. A paused FediDB instance stays paused:
  discovery never turns it back on.
- **Sync now** syncs one instance immediately.
- Only instances **added by hand** can be removed. A FediDB instance would be
  added back by the next discovery run, so pause it instead.
- **Check FediDB now** runs discovery immediately. If FediDB is unreachable the
  error is shown and discovery retries within the hour; tracked instances keep
  syncing.

Cached content still ages out under
[`REMOTE_CACHE_RETENTION_DAYS`](/reference/environment/) once an instance stops
being synced.

<Callout type="note" title="What it sends">
  Discovery and syncing are plain HTTPS GET requests to FediDB and to the tracked instances, identifying themselves as
  `Omicron/<version> (+https://your-domain)`. Nothing about your users is sent. Turn both switches off if your instance
  should never contact a server on its own.
</Callout>

## What it does not change

- **Unpublished posts are never federated.** Only published posts are
  delivered — a draft or a post waiting for its scheduled time is not, and a
  scheduled post federates when it goes live, not when it is scheduled.
- **Private accounts still gate their followers** — approval is enforced before
  anything is delivered.
- The API and your reading experience are unchanged.

## Turning it back off

Flip the toggle and restart. The ActivityPub code paths stop being loaded
entirely. Remote followers will simply stop receiving deliveries; they are not
notified, and their follow records remain on their side.

## Moderation across instances

Once you federate, you inherit the wider network's problems. Two tools:

- **Admin → Federation** suspends or limits a domain, and blocks single remote
  accounts.
- **Admin → Reports** is the moderation queue for user-reported posts and actors,
  local or remote.

See [Moderation tools](/using/moderation/).

## Deliverability notes

- Delivery runs through a **queue**, so a slow or down remote server never blocks
  a user's request. With Redis configured, pending deliveries survive a restart.
- Fedify handles HTTP signatures and retries.
- A remote server that has blocked your domain will reject deliveries; that is
  their decision and there is nothing to fix on your side.
